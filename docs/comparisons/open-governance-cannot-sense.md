## TL;DR

Summarizes themes used in the sibling analysis (**OpenGovernance-reported**; read off-repo for wording; not independently verified here).

- **Core claim:** Human and institutional **senses** (and dashboards built from them) miss harms that are **odourless**—especially in AI: edited model weights, edge-case divergence, agents following poisoned pages, and metrics that look green while conditions erode.
- **Three poison families:** (1) dangers with **no smell** (safety as removable layer, fractal training variance, agents without stake); (2) poisons that **smell like success** (efficiency metrics, cropped scoreboards, desurgence); (3) poisons **outside the frame** (ontology categories, silo lenses, training-data gaps you cannot see).
- **Mercaptan metaphor:** Invisible dangers can be given an artificial “smell”; the essay asks **who will add smell to AI** and whether we outsource sensing to systems with nothing to lose.
- **Pattern recognition:** Compensating sense—generational “don’t cut that corner,” immune drift, board unease—needs friction, slack, and permission to be wrong; optimisation thinking collapses paradox and erodes those conditions.
- **Board questions:** Closing prompts on un-sensed harms, mercaptan for invisible dangers, provable model identity, overriding “fine” data, cost of green metrics, and who may be wrong safely.
